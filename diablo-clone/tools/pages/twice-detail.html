Twice the Detail

Dungeon of Bones, round 21, step 1 of the 2x conversion: characters, spells and effects at twice the detail; the camera moves in half pixels.

In the game

What changed

  • The screen is now drawn at 540 by 960 (it was 270 by 480). Every character, the hero, monsters, villagers and animals, is rendered with twice the pixels each way: rounder robes and heads, thinner outlines, cleaner faces.
  • Projectiles, sparks and particles move at the finer resolution, so their motion is smoother.
  • The camera moves in half-pixel steps. Before, the ground jumped a whole pixel at a time in an uneven rhythm (1, 1, 0, 1) as he walked, a small stutter. Those steps are now half the size.
  • The floors, walls, trees and buildings are unchanged for now (still drawn at the old resolution, then doubled). That's step 2: they need to be drawn piece by piece as you explore, or a forest would need about 130 MB of memory.
  • Buttons and text are unchanged.

Speed

Frame time per frame, measured on a desktop with the CPU slowed 4 times to stand in for a slow phone (typical frame, after the first seconds).

AreaBeforeNow
Dungeon, full speed2.7 ms4.3 ms
Dungeon, 4x slower CPU14 ms21 ms
Forest, 4x slower CPU (no sprite building)13 ms19 ms
  • A frame at 60 fps has 16.7 ms, so a normal phone has room to spare. On a slow phone the forest is the tightest.
  • New poses take longer to build at twice the detail. To avoid stalls, a pose that isn't built yet shows at the old detail for a moment while the sharp version builds in the background. Cast shadows inside a character are now worked out once per 2 by 2 block, which made building about a quarter faster without a visible difference.
