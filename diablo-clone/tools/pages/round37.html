Monster Deaths

Dungeon of Bones, round 37, the last step of the monster plan. Kills now have weight: bodies are knocked back by the blow, monsters die in their own way, big hits burst small monsters apart, and the last kill of a fight lands in slow motion.

Before and after

What changed

  • Knocked back by the blow. A dying monster slides back about two thirds of a tile along the hit, more than a tile for a crit or a finisher. It hops once and lands with a thud of dust. It always falls over backwards, away from you. Bosses barely move.
  • Each family dies its own way.
    • Skeletons: collapse into their heap of bones as before, but the skull now pops off and bounces away.
    • Fallen, diggers, bandits: their spear, pick or staff spins out of their hands and clatters onto the floor.
    • Hounds and wolves yelp, fallen squeal, spiders hiss, and skeletons clatter.
    Dropped skulls and weapons stay on the floor where they land.
  • Overkill. A crit or a finisher that does far more than the monster had left bursts it into chunks and a spray of blood (green ichor for spiders), with nothing left to lie there. Skeletons already shatter, so they don't burst.
  • The last kill. When you kill the last awake monster nearby, and it's the third kill within ten seconds, the game drops into slow motion for half a second.

Fire still burns monsters to ash and cold still shatters them; those take priority over an overkill. COMBAT: OLD on the title screen brings back the old deaths.

Tested

A new test kills each family both ways. It checks that each body slides back and hops, that the weapon or skull flies and is pressed into the floor, that overkills leave no body even for a frame, and that the slow motion starts on the third kill, not before. The other tests and the full run through every level are listed in the notes.
