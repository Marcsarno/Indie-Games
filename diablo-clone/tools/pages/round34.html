Monster Attacks

Dungeon of Bones, round 34, step 1 of making monsters fun to fight. Every attack now has three beats you can read: a wind-up, a strike that lunges in, and a recovery where they're open.

In the game

The poses

How each one attacks

  • Fallen, goblins, diggers, bandits: crouch with the spear raised overhead, leap in about 1.3 tiles, stab down as they land, then stay crouched for a moment.
  • Skeletons: raise the blade high behind the head, step in, chop down hard. The blade stays low afterwards.
  • Hounds, wolves, bears: coil low on the haunches, lunge about 1.6 tiles at full stretch, bite, then hop back out of reach.
  • Spiders: rear up with the front legs raised, then drive down onto you.

What it changes in a fight

  • You can see it coming. About half a second passes between the wind-up and the blow. A small gold glint and a low growl mark the start.
  • They dart in. A monster with a lunge starts its attack from farther out (about 2 tiles instead of 1) and covers the gap in the strike, so packs no longer just walk up and stand.
  • Punish the miss. Hit a monster in its recovery and you see OPEN: the hit does 30% more and knocks it out of the attack.
  • Elites and champions follow up. About half the time they strike a second time straight away, with no second wind-up.
  • The pause between attacks is a little shorter, so the longer attacks don't make monsters much less dangerous.

Archers, casters and bosses' special moves are unchanged. The COMBAT: OLD setting on the title screen still brings back the old attacks for comparison.

Tested

A new test times every family: about half a second from wind-up to blow, lunges from just over 2 tiles to arm's length, and a hit in the recovery stops the attack. Elites followed up 5 times in 15 seconds. The behavior, warrior, reaction and boss tests all pass, as does the full run through every level for all three classes.
