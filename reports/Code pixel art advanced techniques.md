# Tables, Timing and Pairwise Judging Sharpen Sprites

The next quality jump for this project's code-drawn sprites will not come from a new renderer. It comes from four upgrades that sit on top of the form shader already in place. **First, materials are told apart by contrast range and highlight shape, not by texture.** At 32–48 px a material gets only 3–4 tones, so steel, chitin, cloth and bone have to differ in value span, thresholded specular, rim light and contact darkening. Each of those is a few lines in the existing per-pixel pass, because every pixel already knows its normal, part and depth. **Second, combat weight comes from how long poses are held.** Slynyrd's 8-direction attacks use 6 poses and run 400, 550 and 800 ms for sword, spear and hammer. The smear frames stay at 50 ms, and almost all the difference sits in the single anticipation and follow-through holds ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 56](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2025/5/23/pixelblog-56-top-down-character-attack-animation)). The knight's current attack is a continuous 300 ms tween with no hitstop and no screen shake. **Third, Diablo's palette lookup tables make most VFX nearly free at 270×480.** A 51×51 blend table is 2.6 KB, and color cycling plus emissive exemptions cost one array read per pixel ([DevilutionX](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/utils/palette_blending.cpp)). **Fourth, the critique loop should choose between candidates pairwise and in swapped order, not by absolute score.** A GPT-4V judge agreed with humans at Pearson **0.804 for pairwise comparison versus 0.454 for absolute scoring** ([MLLM-as-a-Judge](https://mllm-judge.github.io/)). VLMs also miss the overlap and intersection defects that pixel sprites suffer from: the best model scored **74%** on simple overlapping-circle tasks, against near-100% for humans ([arXiv 2407.06581](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06581v4)). Fitting code primitives to a concept image is worth building only as a secondary tool. In Chat2SVG's ablation, the LLM-written structure accounted for most of the quality and the optimizer added little ([Chat2SVG](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16602v2)). One caveat applies throughout: almost every material and animation number below is practitioner convention or a published shader default, not a measurement. No study has tested any of it on code-drawn sprites with human ratings.

## Materials separate by contrast span and highlight shape, not texture

### The tone budget is 3–4 per material, and hue shift is a large-sprite tool

The size constraint governs every material decision. Pixnote's budget is 2 tones at 16 px, 3 at 32 px, 3–4 at 48 px and 4–5 only at 64 px and above. Its count of its own 16×16 assets is the one hard measurement in this area: **76% of main-to-highlight pairs were near-white, and only 2% shifted hue by 8° or more.** Hue shift "starts to pay off" only at 3 or more ramp steps ([pixnote](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/shading)). That refines Slynyrd's well-known +20°-per-swatch rule, which he applies to 9-swatch ramps ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 1](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/1/10/pixelblog-1-color-palettes)). At this project's 43 px, **lightness separates materials and hue shift adds richness only where a material has room for it.** The practical split, which is an inference, is about 15–20° per step for cloth, gold and skin and 10° or less for steel and stone.

Within that budget, the sources agree on how materials differ. Metal needs high contrast and sharp 1–2 px highlights ([pixnote](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/shading)). It also needs light and dark in several bands, because low-contrast metal "can read as rock" ([Pixel Joint](https://pixeljoint.com/forum/printer_friendly_posts.asp?TID=4897); [Pixel Joint](https://pixeljoint.com/Forum/printer_friendly_posts.asp?TID=5178)). The Wesnoth armour tutorial calls counter-reflection "the key to metal shading": put lighter touches inside the shadows, add reflected tints at about 25–30% strength (orange for torch, brown for nearby leather), and use pinpoint highlights sparingly, most of all on brass and bronze ([Wesnoth](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/Armour_Tutorial)). Cloth is low contrast, with soft fold-following shadows and almost no highlight. Wood is moderate contrast along the grain. Skin shadows "lean warm." Gems are very high contrast and saturated, which makes them the closest documented analog for glossy chitin ([pixnote](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/shading)). Fur and hair are drawn as clumps with jagged cluster edges, and at about 32 px "we don't even need to draw ANY strands of fur" ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/6047047)).

The project's material table in `diablo-clone/prototype-simple/index.html` has a single `shiny` flag and reuses ramps across substances: FLESH, CLOTH and TEAM_RED all point at the same red ramp. The table below turns the conventions into per-material parameters for `defMat`. **The parameters are synthesized and unmeasured.** They are starting points to tune with the blind A/B tool.

| Material (project name) | Tones at 43 px | Value span | Hue shift | Specular | Extra rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plate / blade (STEEL, ARMOR, BLADE) | 4–5 | Widest | ≤10°, cool darks, torch-warm lowest band | Hard 1–2 px, near-white | Non-monotonic "horizon band" (below) |
| Dull iron / mail (IRON) | 3–4 | Medium | Cool grey | 1 px glints on a lattice, lit band only | `(x + (y>>1&1)) % 2` dot pattern |
| Gold trim (GOLD) | 4 | High | Strong, orange → yellow | 1 px pale yellow | Pinpoint highlight matters most here |
| Cloth (CLOTH, TEAM_*) | 3 | Low | 15–20° | None | Wider bands; fold shadows, no lines |
| Leather (LEATHER) | 3 | Low–medium | Warm, shadows toward red-purple | 1 px dull, edges only | — |
| Bone / horn (BONE, HORN) | 3–4 | Medium | Cream lights, cool violet shadows | Small, soft | — |

### Six additions to the form shader cover what published toon shaders do

Published toon shaders do a small, consistent set of things, and the project's `buildForm` pass already does the first. Roystan's widely copied Unity shader uses a hard band from N·L (`smoothstep(0, 0.01, NdotL)`) and an ambient floor of 0.4 so shadows never go black. Its Blinn-Phong spec uses gloss 32, squared, and a hard threshold. Its rim term is `1 − N·V` masked by `NdotL^0.1` and thresholded at 0.716 ([Roystan](https://roystan.net/articles/toon-shader/)). Guilty Gear Xrd, the reference AAA cel-shaded game, keeps shading to two tones. Community breakdowns say its art direction comes from **per-region threshold offsets** that force areas such as under the chin into shadow, plus a separately authored shadow color ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2015/07/26/junya-c-motomura-behind-the-scenes-of-guilty-gear-xrd/); [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2567560)). Third-party reimplementations of t3ssel8r's 3D pixel look draw convex creases as *highlight* lines rather than dark ones ([David Holland](https://davidholland.netlify.app/articles/3d-pixel-art-rendering)). Kyriota states the same idea as a pixel-art convention: outlines on sharp edges "as if it's some kind of highlights" ([Kyriota](https://kyriota.github.io/2022/08/02/UnityPixelatedArtStyleInURP/)).

Six additions follow from this. All are inferences about how to apply these sources here, and all are cheap because the sprite buffer already stores normal, part ID and depth for every pixel.

**Per-part threshold bias.** This is the Guilty Gear idea. `buildForm` already adds a per-pixel bias `b[k]`. Use it systematically: about −0.3 under the helmet brim, on inner thighs and on lower legs, so those areas sit in permanent shadow without any real shadow computation.
**Half-vector specular per material.** Replace today's `shiny && dot > 0.93` rule, which is really a bright-Lambert test, with `dot(N, H) > s` on lit pixels. With V = (0, 0, 1), use s ≈ 0.97 for plate and chitin, 0.95 for gold, 0.96 for wet skin and about 0.90 for leather with a duller color. Guarantee at least one spec pixel per metal part by taking the argmax when nothing passes, and never place spec on the outline.
**Overlap ambient occlusion.** Drop one band where a part in front covers the pixel 1–2 px toward the light. This gives contact darkening where an arm meets the torso or a belt crosses a tabard.
**Part-to-part cast shadows.** Shift the occluder's coverage mask 1–2 px down-right, away from the upper-left light, and drop the parts behind it one band. This is how a helmet shadows the shoulders and a shield shadows the arm. It is a screen-space stand-in for the depth-map self-shadowing that Sprite Lamp offers ([Sprite Lamp](https://store.steampowered.com/app/316830)).
**Torch-side rim and cool back rim.** Light the 1 px just inside the outline when the normal faces the nearest torch (`dot(N.xy, torchDir) > 0.5`, `N.z < 0.6`). Put a faint cool step on the side away from all lights, which acts as "an opposing light source that doesn't cast its own shadow" ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/9656067)).
**Convex-crease highlight lines.** Where the existing part-line rule would draw a dark line at a ridge that faces the light, draw one light-tone pixel instead.

For polished plate there is one further option, also an inference from the "bands" and counter-reflection advice. Map the vertical component of the reflected view vector into a non-monotonic ramp: light for ceiling, darkest for a horizon band, mid tone tinted by the floor and torch for below. That produces the chrome "horizon line" that separates steel from grey stone.

### The light pass dithers moving characters, which the craft sources say not to do

One existing choice conflicts with the dithering evidence. Pixel Parmesan advises against dithering animated character sprites, because "dithering softens edge definition and may disrupt the integrity of forms." It reserves dithering for static props and gradients ([Pixel Parmesan](https://pixelparmesan.com/dithering-for-pixel-artists)). The project's lighting pass quantizes light into 5 bands with a screen-anchored 4×4 Bayer matrix for every pixel, character pixels included. The inference from the code is that when a character moves, the fixed screen-space dither pattern slides across its body, a source of crawl on top of the flicker that Dead Cells never solved ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)). The fix is to light character pixels (the `chr` mask already exists) with a single band sampled at the sprite's chest, or to anchor the dither to sprite-local coordinates. Keep the Bayer dither for floors and walls. For temporal stability more generally, add hysteresis to band thresholds per part, or snap normals to a fixed set of about 16 directions, so bands do not crawl between animation frames.

Readability against dark floors rests on one invariant: "an outline should always increase contrast, and never decrease it." On dark backgrounds "only the darkest shades or black may hold up," so the black exterior outline stays, and colored selective outlining belongs only on interior lines ([Lospec](https://lospec.com/articles/pixel-art-outlines-part-2-using-color)). Cryptark found that specular and glow maps were "especially useful for making enemies pop out from the background" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-visualizing-i-cryptark-s-i-2d-sci-fi-world)). Slynyrd keeps the closest plane "the most saturated with strongest contrast" ([Slynyrd blog](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog)). For this game, that means 1–2 px glints on metal and emissive eyes on monsters as beacons, saturated mid-ramp colors on characters, and desaturated ramps on floors.

## Combat weight lives in hold durations, and the hit frame is data

### Six poses with explicit holds beat a continuous tween

The best low-resolution attack reference is Slynyrd's top-down 8-direction tutorial. Every attack has 6 frames, and the timings are explicit ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 56](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2025/5/23/pixelblog-56-top-down-character-attack-animation)).

| Weapon | Anticipation | Smear (+ rebound) | Follow-through | Recover | Total | Contact point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sword | 100 | 50, 50, 50 | 100 | 50 | 400 ms | ≈62% |
| Spear | 200 | 50, 50, 50 | 150 | 50 | 550 ms | ≈64% |
| Hammer | 250 | 50, 50 + 50 rebound | 300 | 100 | 800 ms | ≈44–50% |

The contact column is my calculation, assuming the hit lands at the start of follow-through. It agrees with Diablo 1's data tables, where the Warrior's hit lands on frame 9 of 16 with a sword and 10 of 20 with an axe, 50–70% of the way through ([DevilutionX animations.tsv](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/assets/txtdata/classes/warrior/animations.tsv)). Diablo II formalizes this as an "action frame" on a 25 fps clock: the hit resolves when playback reaches that frame, and attack speed works by removing whole 1/25 s frames ([Maxroll](https://maxroll.gg/d2/resources/breakpoints-animations); [D2R IAS calculations](https://d2r-ias-calculator.pages.dev/calculations)). Slynyrd's craft rules complete the picture. Smears stay off anticipation and recover frames, because they "noisily distract." Follow-through is one frame pushed 1 px further into the target. "Usually a single smear frame can get the job done," and a color gradient inside the smear adds smoothness without adding frames ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 9](https://slynyrd.com/blog/2018/9/8/pixelblog-9-melee-attacks); [Pixelblog 56](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2025/5/23/pixelblog-56-top-down-character-attack-animation)). Dead Cells, whose characters are about 50 px tall, works pose-to-pose with "the least amount of frames possible." It retimes by dragging keys and once nerfed an overpowered weapon simply by slowing its animation ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)).

The knight's chop (`knight.atk += dt / 0.3`, hit at 0.55) is a continuous 300 ms tween. It draws a trailing crescent through the whole arc. The inferred fix is to turn the attack into a list of `(pose, holdMs)` keys with an `actionFrame` that fires damage, hitstop, VFX and sound from one timestamp. Draw the smear as an annulus sector from the previous blade angle to the current one, only on the 2–3 strike keys, with a white leading edge fading to the weapon color. On mobile, keep the basic attack's anticipation at 100 ms or less, since too much wind-up "will give a nasty feeling of input lag" ([Uppsala devblog](https://babel.speldesign.uu.se/?p=21926)), and put the weight into the post-contact hold instead. Attack-speed bonuses should shorten the holds, not the 33–50 ms smears.

### Hitstop, shake and flash have concrete recipes; ARPG-specific numbers do not exist

Sakurai's eight hitstop techniques are the most actionable source on impact. Shake the victim more than the attacker. Shake horizontally on the ground. Decay the shake. Set hitstop per move. Keep the attacker creeping slightly during the freeze ([Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2022/12/12/this-week-in-sakurai-12-5-12-11-fine-tuning-hit-stop-and-cheating-the-system/)). Smash scales hitlag with damage: a 15% move gets 8–15 frames depending on the game, capped at 20–30 frames at 60 fps ([SSB Wiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)). Hitstop also lengthens cancel windows, and Street Fighter 2's froze both characters for "like 10 frames" ([critpoints](https://critpoints.net/2017/05/17/hitstophitfreezehitlaghitpausehitshit/)). For screen shake, implementations of Eiserloh's trauma model add trauma in [0, 1], decay it linearly, shake by trauma squared, and sample smooth noise per channel. Bevy's official example uses decay 0.5/s, exponent 2, a maximum of 10° and 20 px, noise speed 20 and 0.4 trauma per event ([Bevy](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/examples/camera/2d_screen_shake.rs)). Nijman's "Art of Screenshake" took a dull shooter through about 30 small tweaks, including shake slightly opposite the direction of fire, until it "looked like a commercial game" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)).

Hit-flash and knockback numbers are weaker. A 1–2 frame white flash is generic advice ([uhiyama-lab](https://uhiyama-lab.com/en/notes/unity/unity-game-feel-hit-feedback/)). Vampire Survivors' 120 ms knockback is tagged "[Verify]" on its own wiki ([VS Wiki](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Knockback)). No primary source gives Diablo or Hades values. The recipe below is therefore inference, scaled to a 43 px character on a 270×480 screen.

**Hitstop.** Freeze both pose clocks for 50 ms on light hits, 80–100 ms on heavy hits and 120–200 ms on crits and kills. Jitter the victim ±1–2 px horizontally with decaying amplitude, and run the attacker's pose at about 10% speed.

**Crowds.** In Diablo-sized crowds, apply hitstop per entity. Allow at most one global freeze per 250 ms, on crits and kills only.

**Screen shake.** Scale shake to 4–6 art pixels with no rotation, which would break the pixel grid. Round the offset to whole pixels. Shake when the player takes a hit or on big slams, not on every hit the player deals, and offer a reduce-shake setting.

**Hit flash.** The current 120 ms flash is about 7 frames, so cut it to about 50 ms of white, followed by 1–2 tinted frames on heavy hits.

**Knockback and stagger.** Use 100–150 ms of ease-out knockback over 3–8 px. Add a poise counter so not every hit staggers.

**Death.** Either knock the body back and collapse it in 2–3 stepped rotations, or dissolve it through a fixed Bayer threshold. The threshold is per pixel rather than random, so the dissolve stays stable. Then bake the corpse into the floor layer.

### Two clocks and springs keep computed poses from shimmering

Pixel art is conventionally animated around 12 fps, and engines such as Spine and Unreal are switched to stepped interpolation for pixel sprites, because tweening sub-pixel changes looks mushy ([Divoom](https://divoom.com/blogs/setup-ideas/pixel-art-animation-frame-rate); [Esoteric forum](https://de.esotericsoftware.com/forum/d/3554-issues-with-quotpixel-perfectquot-animation)). Hyper Light Drifter shows the cost of tying logic to frames: moving from 30 to 60 fps meant combing "through over 115 thousand lines of code" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/hyper-light-drifter/hyper-light-drifter-60fps-patch)). The design that follows is an inference.

**Simulation clock.** Input, hit detection, springs, particles and camera run at a fixed 60 Hz, timed in milliseconds.

**Pose clock.** The pose clock samples the pose function only at stepped times, `tPose = floor(t / step) * step`: 33–50 ms on smears, 83–125 ms on walk and idle, and explicit holds elsewhere. Hitstop then just pauses the pose clock while rendering continues.

**Mobile robustness.** Every pose step is at least 33 ms, so a phone that drops to 30 fps shows identical poses.

For secondary motion, t3ssel8r's second-order dynamics is the most reusable single tool. It has three intuitive parameters: frequency f, damping ζ and initial response r, where r > 1 overshoots and r < 0 anticipates. It also includes a stability clamp, `k2_stable = max(k2, dt²/2 + dt·k1/2, dt·k1)` ([chemicalcrux](https://chemicalcrux.github.io/vrc-docs/docs/code/best.crux.animator-dynamics/sources/second-order-dynamics); [Andicraft gist](https://gist.github.com/Andicraft/ab605dda924a0321e755abf8f573ebd7)). The gist has no stated license, so reimplement it rather than copy it. Starting presets, which are unsourced:

| Use | f (Hz) | ζ | r |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knight's scarf or tabard tip | 2–3 | 0.5 | 0 |
| Weapon lag | 5 | 0.7 | 1 |
| Body recoil on hit | 7 | 0.35 | 2 |
| Camera follow | 2 | 1.0 | 0 |

The pixel-stability rules are inference from the Dead Cells flicker admission and forum advice on sub-pixel jitter ([Kirupa](https://forum.kirupa.com/t/best-way-to-stop-subpixel-jitter-in-a-pixel-art-canvas-camera/681011)). Compute in floats. Round every joint once, just before drawing. Draw limbs with a fixed line algorithm. Move a joint's pixel only when its float has moved more than about 0.6 px past the current integer. Quantize idle breathing to 2–3 states. Walks should follow the standard contact–down–passing–up keys with a 1 px bob. Drive gait phase by distance traveled rather than by time (today `walk += dt * 11`), so feet do not slide when haste or slow effects change speed. Add about ±5° of hysteresis at the 22.5° sector boundaries, so diagonal input does not flicker between the planned 8 facings. When mirroring, mirror the rig, not the pixels: in 8 directions "the weapon stays in the same hand" ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 56](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2025/5/23/pixelblog-56-top-down-character-attack-animation)).

## Diablo's lookup tables make VFX and atmosphere nearly free

### Light tables, blend tables and cycling are a few kilobytes of data

DevilutionX's source shows exactly how Diablo got glow, transparency and animated lava from 8-bit indices. Its 16 light tables are index remaps built by sliding each color down its own ramp. Ramp blocks run 16 or 8 colors long, and table 15 is "pitch black." Falloff is linear per light radius in 1/8-tile units, while the Nest and Crypt levels use quadratic falloff "with over-exposure." There are no runtime square roots, because sub-tile distances come from a precomputed `LightConeInterpolations[8][8][16][16]` table ([DevilutionX light_tables.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/engine/light_tables.cpp)). Lava indices are set to identity in every table, "Make the lava fully bright," so hazards stay visible in darkness. Hell levels rotate entries 1–31 of every light table each tick, which animates the blood walls through the lighting itself ([light_tables.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/engine/light_tables.cpp); [lighting.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/lighting.cpp)). Transparency uses a 256×256 table in which each entry is the nearest palette color to the RGB average of two colors, drawn as `dst = LUT[dst][src]` ([palette_blending.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/utils/palette_blending.cpp); [dun_render.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/engine/render/dun_render.cpp)). Diablo II's PL2 files added precomputed 25/50/75% alpha, additive and multiply tables. Three sources describe the block layout differently, but all agree that the tables exist ([OpenDiablo2 docs](https://download.plaud.ai/OpenDiablo2/pl2); [AbyssEngine](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/OpenDiablo2/AbyssEngine/pkg/fileformats/pl2file); [d2mods](https://d2mods.info/forum/viewtopic.php?p=22693)).

Color cycling is the animation layer on top. DevilutionX's `CycleColors` rotates the palette by one index and rotates the blend table's rows and columns with it, so transparency stays correct. Crypt lava (15 colors) cycles in reverse every other frame while its glow (16 colors) cycles every frame, and the source notes "the full animation has 240 frames before it loops." The hive pond's 8- and 7-color ramps loop after 56 frames ([palette.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/engine/palette.cpp)). **Coprime ramp lengths give long, non-repeating loops from tiny data.** Mark Ferrari used cycling for rain, fog, waterfalls and smoke in single-palette scenes ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023586/8-Bit-8-Bitish-Graphics)). Joe Huckaby's HTML5 port found a full 307,200-pixel redraw too slow in 2010 browsers and redrew only the pixels that use cycling indices ([EffectGames](https://www.effectgames.com/effect/article-Old_School_Color_Cycling_with_HTML5.html)).

At 51 colors these tables are tiny (inference). A 16-level light table is 816 bytes. Each 51×51 blend table is 2,601 bytes, so 50%, additive, multiply, 25% and 75% total about 13 KB. They can be built at boot with nearest-color search in OKLab. Use additive for fire, sparks and magic cores, 50% for smoke, ghosts and wall cutaway, and multiply for blood pools and scorch. True alpha is the wrong default: at 270×480 on a high-DPI phone, each art pixel covers 3–4 screen pixels, so a checkerboard reads as a visible pattern. Ordered dither belongs only in dissolves, where the pattern itself reads as disintegration.

### Effect recipes: brief and additive for flashes, persistent for decals

The cited effect evidence is mostly structural. One explosion breakdown uses four emitters (large burst, small burst, large smoke, small smoke), with smoke animating "from fire colors to black, then dissolves" ([r/PixelArtTutorials](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/PixelArtTutorials/comments/1hkuqv1/breakdown_of_this_pixel_art_explosion_effect)). Smoke "puffs out, grows slightly over a frame or two, then breaks into small clouds that vanish" ([Uppsala devblog](https://babel.speldesign.uu.se/?p=33469)). Eight frames is a common pack budget per effect ([1-Bit VFX Pack](https://sincpc.itch.io/1-bit-vfx-pack1)). Saint11's roughly 100 GIF tutorials cover fire, explosions, impacts and blood, but they are image-only and could not be mined for numbers ([saint11.art](https://saint11.art/blog/pixel-art-tutorials/)). Brogue gives torches min and max radii, and a reimplementation swapped random variance for Perlin noise to make it smoother ([anderoonies](https://anderoonies.github.io/2020/11/14/brogue-generation-3.html)). The project's torch flicker, a sum of three sine waves seeded per light, already follows that smooth-noise approach.

The following are conventions and design proposals, not measurements.

**Fire.** A Doom-fire heat buffer of about 128 cells per torch, mapped onto the fire ramp. A cheaper alternative is to cycle 5–6 fire indices in a static flame.

**Hit sparks.** 4–10 particles at 60–120 px/s, living 0.15–0.3 s, drawn additive. Embers: 1–3 per second per torch, stepping down the ramp.

**Smoke.** 50%-blend circles of 3–8 px that grow and rise, then dissolve through a Bayer threshold over the last 30% of their life.

**Explosions.** A 1–2 frame additive flash disk, 4 frames of fireball, a smoke ring with debris, and a scorch decal at the end.

**Blood.** A 60–90° spray cone that stamps into a persistent floor layer. The project already does this with `stampBlood`.

**Lightning.** Midpoint-displaced polylines redrawn every 2–3 frames.

**Shockwaves.** 1 px isometric ellipses (rx = 2·ry) growing for 150–250 ms.

For clarity, rank effect brightness: player-threatening hazards get the brightest values and a unique hue, the player's own attacks are medium and warm, and ambient decor never uses the top of any ramp. Cap particles at about 300 and drop ambient ones first.

### Environment richness comes from seeded variation, region cutaway and three-state fog

Slynyrd's tile guidance gives the base rules. Grout goes first, then bricks, then aging and damage on both. Wall tops are brighter than faces. Every heavily used texture needs at least one variant, plus occasional cracked or discolored tiles "to fight visual fatigue" ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 45](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2023/7/21/pixelblog-45-bricks-walls-doors-and-more)). Boris the Brave's autotiling roundup gives the edge logic. The blob scheme reads all 8 neighbors, and its weighted sum takes only 47 of the 256 possible values, so a 256-entry lookup table selects one of 47 tiles. Sub-blob sets get more variety from 20 subtiles ([Boris the Brave](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2013/07/14/tileset-roundup/)).

The design proposal is to hash each cell, `hash(x, y, levelSeed)`, into variant index, 15% cracks, 8% moss (only next to walls or water), 5% rubble or bone, and a ±1-band brightness offset. Use low-frequency value noise so grime clusters. Bake ambient occlusion at wall bases by dropping 1–2 bands within 2–4 px of a wall edge. Place props by rules: against walls and in corners, never on corridor centerlines, at about 1 per 6–12 room tiles.

Diablo's wall cutaway is per room, not per tile. Generation assigns each room a `TransVal` region. At render time, a wall tile flagged `Transparent` blends only if its region is active. The `Left` and `Right` masks blend only the upper triangle of a wall piece, while "the lower 16 rows are opaque," which keeps the room boundary readable ([gendung.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/levels/gendung.cpp); [dun_render.hpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/engine/render/dun_render.hpp)). Readability is the reason cutaway exists. When D2R's new graphics left some walls opaque, players complained about losing sight of monsters ([Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d2r/t/in-d2r-graphics-certain-walls-arent-transparent-when-your-character-is-behind-them/160254)).

Fog of war is separate from lighting. `DoVision` crawls up to radius 18 from at most 4 vision sources and marks tiles visible and explored. Lights un-light and re-light their area only when `hasChanged`, using radius+2 so diagonal moves leave no stray tiles ([lighting.h](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/lighting.h); [lighting.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/lighting.cpp)). For this game (inference), use three tile states: black for unexplored, a fixed dark band for explored but not visible, and lit for visible. Recompute the crawl when the player changes tile, not every frame.

### Reserve hues and make loot colors differ in value, not just hue

In dark palettes, readability comes from value contrast and linework more than hue. Darkest Dungeon's Chris Bourassa credits strong linework with letting him add "unexpected hits of color" without losing readability ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com:443/articles/the-gothic-sensibilities-of-darkest-dungeon/1100-6424880/)). Diablo II's loot code (blue for magic, yellow for rare, with white, green and gold in community lists) works because it is limited and applied to text labels ([Battle.net](https://classic.battle.net/diablo2exp/items/magic.shtml)). David Brevik traced the scheme "in a way" to Angband ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2019/05/18/the-surprising-origins-of-loot-rarity-colors)). Near-hue tiers are a documented failure: D2R players and colorblind Diablo IV players complained that gold and unique items were hard to tell apart ([Blizzard D2R forum](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d2r/t/bad-text-colors-for-items-rare-unique-and-runes/7096); [Blizzard D4 forum](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d4/t/colorblind-person-here-please-change-the-text-colour-of-uniques-on-the-ground/174380)).

The proposal for a 51-color palette (inference): reserve one saturated family each for enemy danger (red-orange), player magic (blue-cyan), poison (acid green) and loot (gold), and keep environment ramps desaturated. Separate gold from yellow by value and a label border shape as well as by hue. Mark hazard, eye and loot-beam indices as emissive, so every light table maps them to themselves, as Diablo does for lava.

### Mobile 60 fps is plausible but unmeasured, and the current light pass is the risk

At 270×480 (129,600 px), everything above is feasible if it is table-driven and incremental. That conclusion is supported by Diablo's design and by Huckaby's port, but **no 2025–2026 mobile benchmark was found**. The project README reports about 8 ms per frame in headless desktop Chromium. The current lighting pass does per-pixel, per-light 3D distance, an RGB multiply and a 15-bit LUT snap for every pixel, every frame. My inference is that a mid-range phone CPU will run this slower than a desktop, leaving little of the 16.7 ms budget for new VFX.

DevilutionX avoids that cost in three ways. It specializes tiles into fully lit, fully dark, one table per tile, or per-pixel lighting. It precomputes falloff. It recomputes lights only when they change ([dun_render.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/engine/render/dun_render.cpp); [lighting.cpp](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX/blob/master/Source/lighting.cpp)). The proposed final pass is `out32[i] = palRGBA[light[lvl[i]][idx[i]]]`, estimated at 1–3 ms (unmeasured). With it, palette cycling costs nothing, and a torch that changes band 8–12 times per second needs about 10 light recomputes per second, not 60. Avoid per-pixel `Math.random`, `globalAlpha`, `getImageData`, and particle allocation. Today particles use `push` and `splice`; switch them to pooled typed arrays.

## Pairwise, order-swapped judging should replace absolute scores for choosing

### The numbers favor comparison by a wide margin

The project's 5-question yes/no rubric, scored over up to 3 passes, keeps "the best-scoring pass." That is an absolute-scoring selection rule. The 2024–2026 evidence measures it against pairwise comparison, and pairwise wins in every study found.

| Study | Absolute / pointwise | Pairwise | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| MLLM-as-a-Judge, GPT-4V agreement with humans ([project page](https://mllm-judge.github.io/)) | Pearson 0.454 | 0.696 with ties, 0.804 without | Batch ranking worst; the arXiv version gives slightly different values, same ordering ([arXiv 2402.04788](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04788)) |
| Same benchmark, consistency over 6 runs | 0.611 | 0.675 | Batch ranking 0.418 |
| GenArena, correlation with LMArena human leaderboard ([arXiv 2602.06013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06013)) | Spearman 0.36 | 0.86 | Pairwise raised accuracy by over 20%; open judges beat proprietary ones under pairwise |
| GenAI-Arena, naive VLM vote imitation ([NeurIPS 2024](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/97878)) | — | Best 49.19% accuracy | Naive protocols are weak; protocol design matters |

The same benchmark measured the biases that make absolute scores untrustworthy. Gemini, LLaVA and CogVLM mostly gave around 4 out of 5. GPT-4V and Gemini added 0.6–0.75 points to longer answers with the same meaning. GPT-4V slightly preferred its own outputs. LLaVA copied the example ordering 88.2% of the time ([MLLM-as-a-Judge](https://mllm-judge.github.io/)). Pairwise judging has its own position bias: VLM predictions change "significantly" when image order is swapped ([CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Tian_Identifying_and_Mitigating_Position_Bias_of_Multi-image_Vision-Language_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)). The standard mitigation is to judge both orders and report the flip rate ([AesRM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.28078); [IJCNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18.pdf)). Swapping does not fix everything. A 2026 preprint finds VLM judges sometimes "judge without seeing," preferring informative-sounding text over what the image shows ([arXiv 2604.17768](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17768)). That argues for presenting candidates as unlabeled panels with no code or commentary attached.

Juries help in text. Three smaller models from different families beat GPT-4 alone on Cohen's kappa: 0.763 versus 0.627 on NQ and 0.906 versus 0.841 on TriviaQA ([PoLL, arXiv 2404.18796](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.18796)). No image-sprite replication exists. For a single-model Claude workflow, the inferred analog is a majority over several samples or prompt framings, which the 0.675 run-to-run consistency figure justifies.

### Let the code own geometry, because the judge cannot see it

The most actionable blind-spot result is "VLMs are blind." On simple 2D primitives (two overlapping circles, line intersections, nested squares, counting), VLMs averaged 58.57% across 7 tasks. The best model, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, reached 74.01%, later versions reached 77.84%, and humans score near 100%. Models "cannot reliably tell whether two lines (or two circles) are intersecting, especially when close together" ([arXiv 2407.06581](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06581v4)). Those are precisely this engine's failure modes: whether two parts overlap, whether an internal line appears where similar-colored parts meet, whether the outline has a 1 px gap, whether a spec glint landed on the outline.

The inference is a clean division of labor. The renderer knows every part's geometry exactly, so it should emit metrics for those checks from the part-ID and depth buffers. The VLM should judge only gestalt: light consistency, silhouette read and pose energy. That division matches the first report's finding that tests alone missed vanished eyes ([dev.to/jucelinux](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)). Each kind of check covers the other's blind spots. No study measures judge accuracy at 64 px or below, or the effect of nearest-neighbor upscaling, so the contact sheet's 4× panels remain a reasonable guess, not a validated setting.

Off-the-shelf aesthetic models should not become gates. The best learned preference model, HPSv3, reaches only 72.8–85.4% preference accuracy across photoreal and illustration test sets, and older models fall further outside their training data: PickScore scores 61.6 on the ImageReward test set ([HPSv3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03789); [hpsv3 PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/hpsv3/)). An audit of LAION-Aesthetics found it encodes one person's taste and favors realistic landscapes and portraits ([arXiv 2601.09896](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09896v4)). In SD-πXL's user study of pixel-art generators (56 participants), automatic semantic metrics and humans disagreed outright: VectorFusion led the automatic semantic score but was ranked first for fidelity only 0.8% of the time ([arXiv 2410.06236](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06236v1)). The inferred long-term metric is the project's own blind A/B votes. Fit a small Bradley-Terry or logistic model over the six lint metrics to learn which ones predict human preference, then retune their "healthy ranges" from data instead of guesses.

### Fitting primitives to a target helps proportions, not style

Chat2SVG is the clearest precedent for "LLM writes primitives, then an optimizer fits them." Claude 3.5 Sonnet wrote an SVG template and rectified it visually, with "two iterations of refinement typically sufficient." SDEdit and ControlNet produced a detailed target, and DiffVG optimized only the shape parameters under an MSE + curvature + IoU loss ([Chat2SVG](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16602v2); [DiffVG](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~tzli/diffvg/diffvg.pdf)). The ablation sets expectations. Image FID was **33.31 for the full pipeline, 47.21 without the LLM template, and 33.45 without optimization**, and Path FID was slightly *better* without optimization ([Chat2SVG](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16602v2)). Structure does most of the work, and fitting adds geometric detail. SD-πXL, the quality ceiling for palette-exact pixel output, takes 1.5 hours on an RTX 4090 and leaves frame-to-frame consistency as future work ([arXiv 2410.06236](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06236v1)). The 2012 non-neural Pixelated Image Abstraction still tied it on fidelity, 49.3% versus 47.1% ranked first, so a plain downsample-and-quantize is a fine way to prepare a target. Geometrize-style hill climbing runs in browser JS and Node today ([geometrize](https://www.geometrize.co.uk/)). Its free-form output of hundreds of anonymous shapes would destroy editability, though.

The recommendation is inference. The knight was already redrawn in code from two generated reference sprites, so a reference-fitting tool has an immediate user. Build a Node hill-climber over the named parameters of a sprite's rest pose: positions ±2 px, radii ±2, palette index from a short list, on/off flags for details. Score it against the reference downsampled and quantized to the palette, using OKLab ΔE, silhouette IoU and the lint penalties. Never fit per frame, or animation consistency breaks. At about 2,000 pixels per evaluation, thousands of evaluations should take seconds, but that is unbenchmarked. Public 2025–2026 reports of agents drawing sprites via code remain anecdotal. The Sprite Fusion post on Opus 5.5 offers only "not perfect, but much better" ([Sprite Fusion](https://www.spritefusion.com/blog/opus-5-5-for-pixel-art-scenes)). This project's A/B tool can produce better evidence than the public literature offers.

Four items should not be adopted. Do not gate sprites on LAION-Aesthetics, HPS, PickScore or ImageReward. Do not run SD-πXL or DiffVG inside the loop. Do not let a VLM verify overlaps or 1 px details. Do not shake the screen on every hit the player deals.

Several recommendations from the first report need revision in light of this research. Its uniform **+20° hue shift per step** should become per-material: 15–20° for cloth, gold and skin, 10° or less for steel and stone, and tone counts of 3–4 at this size, because pixnote measured that hue barely registers on small pieces. Its rule to **"keep the highest-scoring pass"** should become pairwise, swap-checked selection, with the yes/no rubric demoted to a gate. Its **"6–10 frame attack, hit at 55–65%"** guidance stands, but it should be expressed as explicit per-key holds following Slynyrd's 400/550/800 ms structure, plus hitstop, which it did not cover. Its **3D rig with two-bone IK** should drop IK to optional, since distance-driven gait removes most foot sliding at 43 px. Its **light-table plan** should expand to include blend tables, cycling and region cutaway, and should explicitly replace the current RGB-multiply lighting pass for performance reasons. Its **depth-jump internal lines** should gain the convex-crease highlight variant. The first report did not address dithering in the lighting pass, which should now stop on characters.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the project's two tools as complements. The engine knows its geometry exactly: every pixel's normal, part and depth. The VLM judge is weakest on exactly that kind of geometry, overlaps and intersections, and strongest at comparing two whole images. So geometry should be verified by code and gestalt judged in pairs. Material richness, contact shadows, rims and crease highlights all come from the same buffers that make those checks possible. Likewise, nearly every VFX and atmosphere upgrade reduces to a small table indexed by palette color, so the palette's ramp structure becomes the central asset. Designing it per material is worth more than any individual effect.

Two things remain unknown, and both can be measured inside this project. Nobody has measured VLM judging accuracy at 43 px, and nobody has benchmarked a full-screen typed-array pass on current phones. A phone frame-time measurement and about 50 logged A/B votes would turn the most consequential inferences here, the lighting rewrite and the lint thresholds, into evidence for this specific game.
